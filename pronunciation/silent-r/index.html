The silent R

How beer is made. Session 3.

The rule

My tailor is rich. Rich has a real /r/. Tailor has none at all: it ends /ˈteɪlə/, which is the schwa from session 1.

The /r/ sound is one of the hardest sounds in English, and one of the last that English children master. It is not the French r. The French r is made in the throat. The English one is not.

  1. Your lips form a slight o shape.
  2. Your tongue curls up at the sides.
  3. You tighten the pharynx.

And in British English a written r is very often not pronounced at all. Instead it stretches the vowel in front of it.

bat /æ/  →  barley /ɑː/   ·   bar /ɑː/
The r did not disappear. It turned into length.

Two places this happens: before a consonant (barley, wort, hardness, fermentation) and at the end of a word (lager, bitter, flavour).

Session 1 asked what vowel is in that syllable. This session asks whether there is an r there at all. They go wrong separately: you can get the schwa right and still roll an r onto the end of it.

1 · The two words that prove it

DARKER

Two written r's. Neither one is pronounced.

/ˈdɑːkə/   The first r stretches the vowel. The second is a schwa.

BREWER

Two written r's. Exactly one is pronounced.

/ˈbruːə/   The first r starts a syllable, so it is real. The second ends one, so it is not.

An r at the START of a syllable is real
An r at the END of one, or before a consonant, is not

That is the whole lesson, and those two words hold it. Darker shows you the silence. Brewer shows you the border.

2 · The water test

  1. Take a mouthful of water. Do not swallow it.
  2. Say "ah". Still do not swallow.
  3. What you just did to stop the water going down is tightening the pharynx. That is the position.
  4. Now spit the water out and say roast, rye, brew from that same position, with the lips in a slight o.

No water to hand: start to yawn, then stop halfway and hold it.

3 · /w/ or /r/?

The English /r/ is close to the /w/ of weekend, but it is not the same sound. Here is how to feel the difference. Tap any word to hear it.

  1. Hand on your vocal cords. Hold a long wwwww, then a long rrrrr.
  2. There is more vibration on /r/ than on /w/. That is the difference, and you can feel it before you can hear it.

5 · When the silent R comes back

A final r is silent, unless the next word begins with a vowel sound. Then it comes back, to join the two words together. It is called the linking /r/.

The beer was cold.  →  no r, because was starts with a consonant.
The beer_is cold.  →  the r comes back, to join beer to is.
A porter_and a stout.  →  same thing, because and starts with a vowel.

So beer on its own has no /r/ at all, and beer is has one. Same word. The sound belongs to the join, not to the word.

Everything on this page is British English. American English is rhotic: every written r is pronounced, with the tongue curled back. Pick one accent and be consistent.
