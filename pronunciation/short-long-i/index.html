The chip is not cheap

Chip /tʃɪp/ is something you eat. Cheap /tʃiːp/ is what it costs. One is short and one is long.

Two sounds that French does not have

French has one i. English has two, and they make different words.

the short one

/ɪ/

Say a sound halfway between a French i and a French é.

Your lips are relaxed and barely apart.

fish · rich · chip · pickles

the long one

/iː/

Say it as if someone were taking your photograph: "cheese".

Your lips are pulled wide. You can feel the smile.

cheese · yeast · protein · cheap

1 · Find the two positions

  1. Say the French word si. Now say the French word ses. Go back and forth. Your tongue moves a little each time.
  2. Stop halfway between them and hold it. That noise is /ɪ/. Now say fish, chip, milk.
  3. Now smile as if for a photograph and say "cheese". Hold the vowel. That is /iː/.
  4. Say chip, cheap, chip, cheap. Watch your own mouth if you can. The corners of your lips should be moving. If they are not moving, you are saying the same word twice.
If your lips do not move, neither does the meaning.
This is the one you can check without hearing yourself.

2 · Bet on the spelling

You can often predict the sound from the spelling, before you have heard the word at all.

predict SHORT whenpredict LONG when
the word ends in a consonant and has only one vowel letter

It is a tendency, not a law, and there are many exceptions. So do not learn it as a rule you must obey. Learn it as a bet worth making when you meet a word you have never seen.

3 · The words that break it, and two of them are yours

A tendency with exceptions is still worth having, as long as you know which ones bite.

wordthe rule saysit actually is
sievelong (two vowels in -ie-)short: SIV, and it rhymes with give
biscuitlong (two vowels in -cuit)short: BIS-kit
builtlong (two vowels)short: BILT
givelong (ends in a vowel)short: GIV
livelong (ends in a vowel)short: LIV, and it is a different word from leave
sincelong (ends in a vowel)short: SINS

All six break it the same way: the rule bets long and the word is short. It never goes wrong in the other direction. So when you are unsure and the rule says long, that is the moment to check.

Four of those six are the same exception

The e on the end of give, live and sieve was never there to make the vowel long. It is there because an English word cannot end in a bare v. Look at every v-word you know: have, love, glove, leave, sleeve, twelve, above, active. All of them have that e, and it means nothing at all. It is holding a door open.

Since is the same trick with a different letter: a c cannot end a word when it says /s/, so it needs an e too — once, dance, hence, produce, surface.

So there are not six exceptions to learn. There are three: the v words, the c word, and the odd pair built and biscuit.

A few modern words do end in a bare v, and they are all recent: rev, spiv, chav, Slav, Kyiv, Tel Aviv. Clippings and borrowings, every one. The old vocabulary obeys the rule.
