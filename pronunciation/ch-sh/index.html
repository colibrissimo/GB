CH and SH

Food phrasal verbs and idioms. Session 3.

The rule

French has one of these sounds. English has both, and writes them differently.

SH is /ʃ/, the sound of chat and chocolat. In English it is spelt sh: ship, fish, fresh, mushroom. You already make it perfectly.

CH is /tʃ/, and it is two sounds glued together: a /t/ and then the /ʃ/. It is the sound at the start of tchin-tchin and at the end of match. In English it is spelt ch: chips, cheese, chicken, chop.

chips /tʃɪps/   ·   ships /ʃɪps/
One starts with the tongue touching the ridge behind your teeth. The other never touches.

The French trap: you see ch and you say the French ch. So chips becomes ships, cheap becomes sheep, chop becomes shop, and watch becomes wash. Every one of those is a different word.

The letters ch are not always /tʃ/, either. Section 5 has the two exceptions. Both are words you already know how to say, because one set is French and the other is science.

1 · The two words that prove it

CHIPS

/tʃɪps/   The tongue touches first, blocks the air, then lets it go into the sh.

SHIPS

/ʃɪps/   The tongue never touches. The air goes from the start.

CHEF  ·  CHIEF

Same two letters. chef /ʃef/ is a French word, so it keeps the French sound. chief /tʃiːf/ is English, so it takes the English one.

CH = t + sh. Touch, then release.
SH = sh only. Never touch.

2 · How to make the CH

  1. Say a long shhhhh, the one you use to ask for quiet. Notice the tongue is near the ridge behind your top teeth, not on it.
  2. Now put the tip of your tongue on that ridge. Block the air completely for a moment.
  3. Release it straight into the shhh. That release is /tʃ/. Say tchin-tchin. Then cheese.
  4. Hand flat in front of your mouth. CH gives a small puff at the start. SH gives a steady stream and no puff. Test it on chop and shop.

If it sounds like sh with a little click before it, you are already there. The click is the t.

3 · CH or SH?

Each pair is two different words. Tap to hear, then say both with your hand in front of your mouth.

5 · The letters lie, three ways

ch = /tʃ/The English default. chips · cheese · chicken · chop · cherry · chocolate · lunch · kitchen · sandwich
ch = /ʃ/Words English borrowed from French keep the French sound. chef · machine · champagne · quiche · brochure · moustache
ch = /k/Words from Greek, which is most of science. chemistry · chemical · technology · stomach · mechanic · character · ache
/ʃ/ with no shThe sound is there and the letters are not. sugar · sure · nutrition · fermentation · delicious · special
-ture = /tʃə/Session 1's transparent words. mixture · temperature · texture · culture · nature. Not the French -ture.
A short rule that nearly always works: if it looks French, say it French. If it looks like science, say k. Otherwise, touch first.
