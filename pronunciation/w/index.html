The sound W

A letter French calls double-v, and English calls double-u for a reason.

The rule

In French it is double-v. In English it is double-u. As inDUH-bul-YOO.

Round your lips, as if blowing out a candle.
Or starting to whistle. Then let the vowel out.

The one thing to avoid: the teeth. If your top teeth touch your bottom lip you have said a v, and wine has become vine. The lips must not touch the teeth at all.

Check it in a mirror, or with a finger. Hold one finger flat, a centimetre in front of your mouth, and say west. You should feel a small push of air from rounded lips.

2 · When the w says nothing

w before rwrite · wrong · wrist · wrap
The w is silent. Only the r is said. RITE, RONG
wh before owho · whose · whole
Only the h survives. HOO, HOOZ, HOLE
after a vowelgrow · grew · flow · sewage
The w stops being a consonant and stretches the vowel in front of it instead. Sewage is SOO-idj: no w sound at all.
three odd onestwo · sword · answer
No rule.TOO, SORD, AN-sa

which and witch are pronounced exactly the same in British English. So are whale and wail. The h in wh does nothing at all, unless an o follows it.

4 · The sound QU

qu is never just k. It is k plus double-u.

quality quantity liquid equipment sequence frequency aquatic aquifer require adequate

Every one of those is a word you will meet on a contaminated site or in the lab that tests it, and every one of them contains a sound you would never guess from looking at it. Aquifer is AK-wi-fuh, not AK-i-fer, and adequate is AD-i-kwut.

Three more with no w and no qu: one is WUN, once is WUNCE, and choir is KWIRE. In choir there is no w, no u after a q.

5 · Say it

Lips rounded, teeth out of the way. Slowly first, then at speed.
