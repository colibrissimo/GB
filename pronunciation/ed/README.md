# The -ed ending

**Status: built 8 September 2026, for SAB3 S5 session 2 (Present perfect + the
brewing thread, Friday 11 September, 10:00). Audio: 9 of 45 buttons voiced, all 9
inherited. 36 clips to record — see `AUDIO_TODO.txt`. Not yet on Moodle.**

Adapted from the GEII module at `geii/pronunciation/ed/`, and the adaptation was
larger than it looked. Sarah's first read was that the second half needed the work
and the first half was portable. It is the other way round.

- **Section 1** (there is only one mistake) and **section 4** (the -ed adjectives)
  are content-neutral and came across almost untouched.
- **Section 2**, the drill, was the most GEII thing on the page: *calibrate, weld,
  wire, mount, install, convert, plug, charge*. All 22 items replaced.
- **Section 3** kept two general pairs and replaced three.
- **Section 5**, the say-it sentences, was entirely electrical — fuse, board,
  sensor, circuit, capacitor. All four rewritten.

It is also ported forward a generation. The GEII file is the older shell: a
hardcoded `HAVE` manifest of filenames inside the script, a relative clip path, no
cross-repo cascade, and the audio double-fire bug still in it. This is the GB
single-page shell, same as `h` and `silent-r`, so it can be pasted into a Moodle
Page.

## Counts
- **5 sections** plus the rule panel
- **45 listen buttons**, of which **9 cascade in free** from the geii -ed bank
- **24 drill items in two stages**: a core of 16, then 8 extra. 9 of the 24 add a
  syllable, 15 do not
- **4 sentence clips**
- **36 clips still to record**, all of them brewing words

## Where the verbs come from
All 24 are from material the students have already met: the Set 1 process verbs in
`Brewing Process Vocabulary.docx` (*crush, separate, heat, cool, add, transfer,
produce, mature, carbonate*), the `How beer is made` list (*brew, boil, degrade,
metabolise, filter, roast, taste, flavour*), and the process nouns they already
drill as stages (*malting, mashing, boiling, conditioning, packaging*). None was
chosen because it was convenient for the rule, and the page says so at the bottom.

## Decisions taken 8 September 2026
- **The trap is one trap, repeated.** *filtered, transferred, matured, flavoured,
  conditioned, metabolised.* Long words whose ending costs nothing. French speakers
  add a syllable in exactly these, because the word feels like it deserves one. Six
  of the twenty-four are that shape, which is deliberate over-weighting.
- **`produced` is the second trap and it is a different one.** *Produce* ends in
  /s/, so the ending is /t/ and the word does not grow. Students hear the /s/ and
  reach for /ɪd/ because /s/ feels like it needs a vowel after it. It is flagged in
  the drill as "the one to get wrong".
- **`aged` earns section 4 on its own.** It is the only word on the page that is
  both: *the beer aged in oak* is AYJD, one syllable, and *an aged lambic* is
  AY-jid, two. Same five letters, both used on a brewery tour, and it makes the
  adjective exception a brewing fact rather than a list of five words to memorise.
- **NO NAKED FLAMES stays.** It was written for a GEII lab and it is better here.
  Every brewhouse has that sign next to the boiler.
- **The drill stops at sixteen, and the stop is real.** Same reasoning as the `h`
  module: one shuffled pool puts "the end" somewhere different on every load, so
  "the last few are optional" is meaningless and the trap a student most needs can
  land at 22 and be skipped. Core and extra are shuffled separately. The core of 16
  carries all three endings, both long-word traps and the *produce* trap.
- **Two GEII clips kept in section 3.** *it works / it worked* and *it helps / it
  helped* are general English, not GEII vocabulary, and they cascade in with no
  recording. The three brewing pairs beside them do not, yet.
