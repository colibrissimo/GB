The -ed Ending

Three endings, one rule, and one mistake that gives you away.

/t//d//ɪd/
after a voiceless consonant

crushed · mashed · hopped
packed · produced · washed		 after a voiced consonant or a vowel

brewed · boiled · cooled
poured · filtered · flavoured		 only after the sounds t or d

malted · heated
fermented · added
/t/ is voiceless. /d/ is voiced.
So each ending simply matches the sound in front of it.

1 · There is only one mistake

Do not add a syllable.
Unless the verb already ends in a t or d sound, in which case you must.

Brewed is one syllable, not two. Crushed is one syllable, not two. The ending is a single consonant stuck on the end of the word you already had, and nine times out of ten it costs nothing at all.

4 · The exceptions

Some adjectives ending in -ed ignore the rule entirely and take /ɪd/, even though no t or d comes before it.

naked /ˈneɪkɪd/   wicked /ˈwɪkɪd/   sacred /ˈseɪkrɪd/   beloved /bɪˈlʌvɪd/   rugged /ˈrʌɡɪd/

⚠ NO NAKED FLAMES

That sign is on the wall of every brewhouse you will ever stand in, next to the mash tun and the boiler. It is NA-kid, two syllables, not naked rhyming with baked.

The one that matters here is aged, because it is both. The beer aged in oak is the verb: AYJD, one syllable. An aged lambic is the adjective: AY-jid, two. Same five letters, and the brewery tour guide will use both in one sentence.

The test is grammatical, not phonetic: if it is an adjective rather than a past verb, it may take /ɪd/. Compare he learned it (verb, one syllable ending) with a learned professor (adjective, LEARN-id).

5 · Say it

The orange endings are the only ones that add a syllable. Everything else stays exactly as long as it was.

We crushed the malt, mashed it and brewed the beer. (three verbs, no extra syllables) 🔊 Listen
