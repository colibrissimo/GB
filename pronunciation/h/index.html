The /h/ sound

History of beer. Session 1.

The rule

/h/ is not a consonant you place somewhere. There is no tongue position, no lip shape, no vibration. You push a breath out in front of the vowel, the way you fog a window on a cold morning.

French has no /h/ at all. So the muscle is not missing and it is not difficult. It is unused.

eat  →  heat   ·   ate  →  hate   ·   old  →  hold
The vowel does not change. One breath arrives in front of it.

Three problems, not one.

  1. Saying it. hop, harvest, honey, head — the breath is there and French speakers drop it.
  2. Not saying it. hour, honour, honest — the h is written and there is no breath at all.
  3. Not inventing it. ale, ancient, India — having learned that English likes its h, you start putting one where there is none.

The third is the one nobody warns you about, and it is the one that makes ale come out as hale.

1 · The two words that prove it

AN HOUR

Written h. No breath at all. /ˈaʊə/

And you already knew: you write an hour, never a hour. Nobody ever told you why.

ALEHOUSE

No h at the front — and you will put one there. One h in the middle — and you will drop it.

/ˈeɪlhaʊs/   Both mistakes live in one word.

“an” in front means there is no /h/
“a” in front means there is one

The article is a written test for a spoken thing. a hop · a harvest · a history all take a, because the breath is there to keep the two words apart. an hour · an honour · an honest brewer · an heir all take an, because it is not.

One wobble, so it does not surprise you: some British writers still put an in front of historic, and a few older speakers say an hotel. Leftovers from a time when those h's were dropped. Not a rule you need, but you will meet it in writing about beer history.

2 · The window test

  1. Hold your hand flat, a few centimetres in front of your mouth.
  2. Say ate. Nothing lands on your palm.
  3. Say hate. You should feel warm air arrive before the vowel.
  4. Now run the beer words and check every one warms your hand: hop, hops, harvest, honey, head, half.
  5. No warmth means no /h/, and the word did not happen.

No hand free: whisper the word instead. Whispered, the h becomes the loudest part of it.

The trap. These three keep the /h/ and lose the /w/: hoo, hooz, hole.
who whose whole
herb is the one word where the two accents split. British /hɜːb/, with the breath. American /ɜːrb/, without. It matters here: before hops arrived, beer was flavoured with gruit, a mixture of herbs. Say the h — everything else on this page is British.

5 · Do not invent one

The mirror-image mistake, and the one nobody corrects. These words start on a bare vowel. Open your mouth and let the vowel arrive with no breath in front of it.

ale alewife ancient Egypt empire inn oats intoxication ingredient India Pale Ale

IPA is India Pale Ale. Three words, three bare vowels, no h anywhere near it.
