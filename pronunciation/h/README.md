# The /h/ sound

**Status: built 3 September 2026, for SAB3 S5 session 1 (History of beer, Friday 4
September, 08:00). Audio partly recorded — see below. Not yet on Moodle.**

Adapted from the GEII deck at `geii/pronunciation/h/`, not built cold. The GEII
version is a twelve-slide fullscreen deck; this is the GB single-page shell, the
same one as `silent-r`, so it can be pasted into a Moodle Page.

## Counts
- **8 sections** plus the rule panel
- **77 listen buttons**, of which **36 already have a recording** in the GEII H
  bank and cascade in for free
- **10 minimal pairs** — all ten recorded already
- **35 drill items in two stages**: a core of 20, then 15 optional. Across the
  whole pool, 12 have no /h/, 19 have one, 4 have two
- **7 sentence clips** across 6 lines
- **41 clips still to record**, all of them beer words. `AUDIO_TODO.txt` lists them.

## Where the words come from
The beer words are taken from the vocabulary the students actually meet, not from a
planning note: *hop* (Part 1 and Part 2), *headquarters* (Part 1), *alehouse*,
*household*, *inhabitant*, *harvest* (Part 2), *beheaded*, *unhealthy* (Part 3).
The rest — *hops, hoppy, honey, head, half, hogshead, hymn, herb* — are session
words rather than list words, and are marked as such nowhere on the page because
students do not care about the distinction.

**The evidence is thinner than for silent R.** The Part 1 list has exactly two /h/
items in its answer column (*hop*, *headquarters*). This module is not justified by
frequency in one list, and it should not be claimed that it is. It is justified by
the failure being systematic — French has no /h/ at all — and by *hop* being the
word the whole semester turns on. That argument is in `Pronunciation_decisions.md`.

## The three problems, in order
1. **Saying it** — /h/ does not exist in French, so the gesture is unused rather
   than difficult. *hop, hops, hoppy, harvest, honey, head, heat, half.*
2. **Not saying it** — h written and silent. *hour, honour, honest, heir*, the
   middle-h set carried over from GEII, and *what, when, where, why*.
3. **Not inventing it** — the over-correction. *ale, alewife, ancient, Egypt,
   empire, inn, oats, ingredient, intoxication, India Pale Ale.*

## Decisions taken 3 September 2026
- **The article is the anchor, not a minimal pair.** *an hour* / *a hop*. It is a
  written test for a spoken thing, students already write it correctly, and none of
  them has been told why. Silent R got *darker*; this gets *an hour*.
- **Second anchor is `alehouse`**, because both errors live in one word: no h at the
  front, where they will add one, and one h in the middle, where they will drop it.
  It is also a Part 2 vocabulary item.
- **`who`, `whose` and `whole` are filed as sounding their h**, against the other
  wh- words, same as the GEII deck. Called out in a key box rather than left as a
  trap in the drill.
- **`herb` is flagged as the accent split** — British /hɜːb/ with the breath,
  American /ɜːb/ without. It earns its place here because gruit, the herb mixture
  used to flavour beer before hops, is part of the session's history.
- **`an historic` is named as a wobble, not taught as a rule.** They will meet it in
  written beer history and it contradicts the article test, so it is better disarmed
  than discovered.
- **The drill stops at twenty, and the stop is real.** Sarah's instinct on 3
  September was to keep all 35 and tell students the ones at the end were optional.
  That could not work as built: the pool was one shuffled list, so "the end" was a
  different fifteen words on every load, and *household* or *an honest hour* could
  land at 33 and be skipped by half the room. Split into a core of 20 and an extra
  of 15, shuffled separately. The core carries both anchors, the who/whole trap,
  all three problems and all three counts, so a student who stops at the twenty has
  met the whole lesson.
- **Drill counts breaths, not letters**, so *household* and *hogshead* are two,
  *alehouse* is one, and *an honest hour* is none at all.

## Audio provenance
The 36 inherited clips were made by Sarah on TTSmp3.com for the GEII deck in August
2026 — **not** `say`-generated, so do not assume the offline pipeline for the rest.
The 40 new ones are unrecorded as of 3 September. Until they exist the page falls
back to the browser voice, which is usable in class but flat, and will be American
unless the machine has a British voice installed. On an American voice *herb* comes
out without its h, which is the one word on the page where that matters.

## What is missing
1. **The 40 beer clips.** Everything else on the page is already voiced.
2. **Imagery.** None, same as the GEII deck.
3. **A `hop`-versus-nothing pair.** The ten inherited pairs are general English
   (*art/heart*, *eat/heat*). No beer word has a real minimal partner: *ops* is not
   a word. Padding one in was refused.
