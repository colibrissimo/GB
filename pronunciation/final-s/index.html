The final S

What is biotechnology? Session 4. In French the final s is silent. In English it is not.

One rule, three grammars

The pronunciation rule is the same for the plural, the genitive and the third person singular.

two cells  ·  the scientist's report  ·  it grows

There are three possible pronunciations. The sound in front decides.

/z//s//ɪz/
after a voiced consonant, and after any vowel

cells · genes · hormones
it grows · it kills		 after a voiceless consonant

pollutants · plants · antibiotics
it detects · it ferments		 after a sibilant
(and it adds a whole syllable)

diseases · processes · viruses
it produces

One rule and one exception:

/z/ is the normal ending.
It becomes /s/ only when the sound before it is voiceless, because you cannot switch your voice on that fast.

Fingers on the throat. Say the end of lab, then the end of lap. /b/ buzzes and /p/ does not.

3 · Why an extra syllable?

/ɪz/ exists to make the /s/ or /z/ audible.

Try it. Say process, then try to add an s straight on the end without changing anything. You cannot. An s after an s is an s. So English pushes a small vowel in front of it, and the word grows a syllable.

one syllable morebecause
process → processesPRO-cess-iz. Three syllables, not two.
disease → diseasesdi-ZEEZ-iz. The written se is already a /z/.
virus → virusesVY-rus-iz.
cell → cellsno. Cell ends in /l/, so cells stays one syllable with a /z/ on the end.
The word that breaks the pattern: species. species is one species and two species. The s is already there, said /z/, and nothing is added. The same for series.

4 · The same rule, wearing an apostrophe

Tap any of them to hear it.

genitivesoundbecause
Mike's cat/s/Mike ends in /k/, voiceless
Rose's cat/ɪz/Rose already ends in a buzz. ROH-ziz
Tess's car/ɪz/TESS-iz. The one everybody gets wrong
the scientist's report/s/scientist ends in /t/, voiceless
Pasteur's lab/z/in English, Pasteur ends in a vowel sound, and vowels are voiced
the scientists' meeting/s/ends in /t/, voiceless. The apostrophe is silent
the cells' membranes/z/ends in /l/, voiced

Notice the last two. The scientists' meeting and the cells' membranes have no extra s written at all, and they are still pronounced with the ending they already had. The apostrophe never makes a sound. It only tells you who owns the thing.
