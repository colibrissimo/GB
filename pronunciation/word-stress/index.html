Word stress

Part 1 of 2: inside the word. Part 2 is the sentence.

The rule

French rhythm is a machine gun. Every syllable the same length, the same force, rat-a-tat-a-tat. English rhythm is Morse code. Strong and weak alternate, and one syllable in each word stands out from the rest.

fer-men-ta-tion  →  four even syllables
fermenTAtion  →  one syllable said, the other three thrown away

What "stressed" actually means: the stressed syllable is higher, stronger, longer. Not only louder. Higher in pitch, stronger in force, longer in time, all three at once.

And the other syllables? They go to the schwa, the weak "uh". Barley is BAR-lee, container is kuhn-TAY-nuh.

The mark in the dictionary. When you look a word up you will see /ˈ/ before a syllable. That is the stress, and it goes in front of the syllable you lean on: aroma is /əˈrəʊmə/, so the stress is on ro. On this page we use CAPITALS instead, because they are easier to see: aroma.

2 · The stress is not in the word. It is in the ending.

Find the ending. Count back one. Done.
You do not need to know the word. You need to know the ending.
-ion
-ial		 Stress the syllable just before it.
fermentation · filtration · carbonation · distillation · germination · production · material · industrial
-ic
-ical
-ity		 Stress the syllable just before it.
aromatic · organic · alcoholic · chemical · biological · acidity · quality · clarity

The ending moves the beat. alcohol, but alcoholic. organism, but organic. Same root, and the stress jumps to wherever the ending tells it to go.

3 · The procedure

Four steps. It works on words you have never seen.

a · cid · -ity
1Find the ending. Here it is -ity.
2That ending is in the "just before" list.
3Count back one syllable from the ending: cid.
4Say it higher, stronger, longer, and throw the others away.
acidity   •●••  (four beats when you clap it: -ity is two)

Try it on a long one: pasteurisation. Ending -ion, count back one, and you get pasteurisation. Louis Pasteur would say it the French way. A brewer in Leeds would not.

4 · Same spelling, two beats

Noun: stress at the front. Verb: stress at the back.
fermenta ferment (the thing) · to ferment (the action)
extractmalt extract · to extract the sugars
producelocal produce · to produce beer
importan import · to import hops
increasean increase · to increase the temperature

In a dictionary, look at the letters in brackets: (n.) and (v.). If both are there, check where the /ˈ/ goes for each.
