The L Sound

French has one. English has two, plus a third case where it is not there at all.

Read this before you try

If you do not get the L right, you will still be understood. What you will not do is sound like a native speaker. And ale said the French way is a different drink.

One letter, two sounds

In English the letter L has a clear version and a dark version, and the difference is where the back of your tongue is. In French, every L is the clear one. That is the whole problem: your L is not wrong, it is just always the same.

clear L

/l/

Where: at the start or in the middle of a word, and before a vowel.

How: exactly your French L. The tip of the tongue touches the ridge behind your teeth. You already own this one.

lager · light · flavour · clear

dark L

/ɫ/

Where: at the end of a word, and after a consonant or a pause.

How: Make a normal clear L, and at the same time raise the back of your tongue as if you were about to say "ou".

ale · full · malt · bottle

A vowel after the L makes it clear. Anything else makes it dark.
That is the entire rule, and it is about the sound AFTER the L, not the L itself.

One word can hold both. Floral: the first L has a vowel after it, so it is clear. The last L has nothing after it, so it is dark. Same letter, twice, in six letters of word. Label does the same thing, and so does herbal with just the one.

In some transcriptions the dark L is written /ɫ/, an L with a line through it. You do not need the symbol. You need the tongue.

1 · Feel the back of your tongue move

Ten seconds, out loud. This is the only thing on the page that cannot be done by reading.

  1. Say "la", slowly, and hold the L. The tip of your tongue is up. The back is flat and low. That is the clear L, and it is your French one.
  2. Now say "ou" on its own. Notice what the back of your tongue does: it lifts and pulls back.
  3. Now do both at once. Tip up for the L, back up for the "ou". Hold it. That noise is the dark L.
  4. Say full. Then say ale. Then say cool. Hold the last sound of each for two seconds and feel that the tongue is doing two things at once.
"A full pale ale, well chilled" 🔊 Listen
Five dark L's in a row. Say it slowly, then say it at speed.

2 · Look at what comes next

One L is picked out in each word. Do not think about the L. Look at what follows it, and the answer arrives on its own.
