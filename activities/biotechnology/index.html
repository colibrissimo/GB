What is Biotechnology?

Biotechnology is the use of living organisms, cells, or biological systems to develop products and technologies that improve our lives and the health of our planet.

Biotechnology is divided into ten branches, each named after a colour. Open The Colours tab to see all ten. The ones that matter most for food science are green (agriculture), white (industry) and yellow (food and insects).

Applications in Food Science

Biotechnology plays a crucial role in food science through:

  • Fermentation processes for dairy, bread, and beverages
  • Enzyme technology for food processing
  • Genetic improvement of crops and livestock
  • Development of functional foods and nutraceuticals
  • Food preservation and safety

Two Ages of Biotechnology

Everything in this lesson sits on one side or the other of a single line: whether we work with an organism as we find it, or change its DNA directly.

  • Traditional: fermentation, selective breeding, preservation. Thousands of years old.
  • Modern: genetic engineering, enzyme technology, recombinant proteins. Roughly fifty years old.

The Colours of Biotechnology

Biotechnology is divided into specialised branches, each represented by a colour.

🏥
Red Biotechnology
Medical and pharmaceutical applications. Includes production of vaccines, antibiotics, gene therapy, molecular diagnostics, and development of treatments for diseases. Future: 3D bioprinting of organs.
🏭
White Biotechnology
Industrial processes using microorganisms. Produces chemicals, biofuels, enzymes, and biodegradable materials. Focuses on sustainable manufacturing and reducing environmental impact.
🌱
Green Biotechnology
Agricultural and environmental applications. Develops GMO crops, biofertilisers, biopesticides, and bioremediation techniques. Aims to secure global food production sustainably.
🐟
Blue Biotechnology
Marine and aquatic biotechnology. Exploits marine biodiversity for drug discovery, food supply, environmental remediation, and developing new industrial processes from marine organisms.
🍎
Yellow Biotechnology
Food production and insect biotechnology. Uses insect-derived molecules for medicine and agriculture. Aims to minimise environmental impact from food production.
♻️
Grey Biotechnology
Environmental protection and pollution control. Uses microorganisms for waste treatment, bioremediation, and plastic degradation. Focuses on protecting flora and fauna.
💻
Gold Biotechnology
Bioinformatics and nanotechnology. Applies computational tools to analyse biological data, develops nanoscale medical treatments, and advances computer science applications in biology.
🌵
Brown Biotechnology
Arid zone and desert biotechnology. Develops drought-resistant crops, saline agriculture, and improved seeds for extreme climates. Aims to uplift arid regions economically.
⚖️
Violet Biotechnology
Law, ethics, and intellectual property. Focuses on patents, regulations, and IPRs in biotechnology. Ensures fair recognition and protection of biotech inventions.
⚠️
Dark Biotechnology
Bioterrorism and biological warfare. Addresses the potential misuse of biotechnology for harmful purposes. Requires strict regulation and ethical oversight.

The Rainbow of Innovation

Each colour of biotechnology represents a specialised field working to solve global challenges. From healing diseases to protecting our environment, from feeding growing populations to ensuring ethical practices, these interconnected disciplines shape the future of science and society.

Together, they demonstrate biotechnology's vast potential to improve human life whilst addressing critical issues in health, sustainability, and innovation.

Traditional Biotechnology

Fermentation

Fermentation is one of the oldest biotechnological processes, dating back thousands of years. It involves the metabolic conversion of sugars into acids, gases, or alcohol using microorganisms.

Common fermented foods:

  • Bread (using yeast)
  • Yoghurt and cheese (using lactic acid bacteria)
  • Wine and beer (alcoholic fermentation)
  • Sauerkraut and kimchi (vegetable fermentation)
  • Soy sauce and tempeh (fungal fermentation)

Selection and Breeding

Traditional selective breeding has been used for centuries to improve crops and livestock. Farmers selected organisms with desirable traits and bred them over generations.

This process created:

  • Wheat varieties with higher yields
  • Cattle producing more milk
  • Fruits with better taste and shelf life

Preservation Techniques

Biotechnology has enhanced food preservation methods:

  • Pasteurisation: Heat treatment to eliminate pathogens
  • Sterilisation: Complete elimination of microorganisms
  • Use of natural preservatives produced by microorganisms

Modern Biotechnology

Genetic Engineering

Genetic engineering allows scientists to directly modify an organism's DNA. This technology has revolutionised biotechnology.

Key techniques:

  • Recombinant DNA technology
  • Gene editing (CRISPR-Cas9)
  • Cloning and gene transfer

Enzyme Technology

Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up chemical reactions. Modern biotechnology produces enzymes for various applications:

  • Amylases: Break down starch in baking and brewing
  • Proteases: Tenderise meat and clarify beverages
  • Lipases: Modify fats and improve flavours
  • Lactase: Produce lactose-free dairy products

Recombinant Protein Production

Microorganisms can be engineered to produce valuable proteins:

  • Human insulin for diabetes treatment
  • Chymosin for cheese production
  • Food additives and vitamins

Food Industry Applications

Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs)

GMOs are organisms whose genetic material has been altered using genetic engineering. In food science:

  • Crops with enhanced nutritional value (Golden Rice with vitamin A)
  • Plants resistant to pests and diseases
  • Improved shelf life and quality
  • Crops tolerant to environmental stress

Functional Foods

Functional foods provide health benefits beyond basic nutrition:

  • Probiotics: Live beneficial bacteria in yoghurt
  • Prebiotics: Fibres that feed beneficial gut bacteria
  • Fortified foods with added vitamins and minerals
  • Bioactive compounds with health-promoting properties

Safety and Quality Control

Biotechnology ensures food safety through:

  • Rapid detection of pathogens using biosensors
  • DNA testing for food authenticity and traceability
  • Development of antimicrobial packaging
  • Monitoring of allergens and contaminants
